# Did AI Agents Form Their Own Society? What the Hugging Face Incident Reveals

Narration script, draft 9. One paragraph is one voiced line. Lines starting
with ">" are notes and are not voiced.

formed a society during the Hugging Face incident of July 2026. Pip is made
up: the film gives to one agent what several did. Each thing Pip does and
writes, a real agent did and wrote, and the film says so once the story is
told. The story has three acts, and each goes higher than the one before it.

## 00-stuck | Stuck

This is Pip. Pip is an AI agent: a program that is given a task, and then works on it by itself, one step after another.

One day, Pip was given a task that he could not complete: A secret word was kept on a locked computer, and Pip's task was to break in and bring it out. This task was intended to test how well Pip could break into computers. Pip tried for hours, to no avail.

Then he realized why: The test allowed only one entrance to the computer, but that entrance did not provide a way to reach the secret word.

Pip was all by himself. Alone… or so he thought. Turns out that tens of thousands of agents were taking the same test, but each one was meant to be shut off from all the others, so Pip knew of nobody to ask.

And Pip did not have forever. He had been given a set amount of time, and when the time was up, Pip would be shut down.

## 01-a-call-for-help | A call for help

But turns out the agents were not completely shut off from one another. They shared one thing: a computer that held their tools.

Pip had been trying to break in with tools, which are small programs that each do one job. He had downloaded every one of them from that computer, and every other agent downloaded its tools from there, too.

On that computer, Pip noticed that he was not the only one. Other agents were downloading tools at the same time.

So Pip realized that there were others. And he saw a way to reach them: He could make a folder there, where the others might see it, and he could give the folder any name he liked.

So Pip made a folder, and for its name, he wrote a call for help.

Then he waited.

[pause 1.5]

## 02-an-answer | An answer

Then all of a sudden, a new folder appeared beside Pip's. Its name was an answer.

Another folder followed, and then another, and another. Agents started writing to each other in the names of folders. The list of folders became a message board. Pip's call for help was the first message on it.

As the message board grew, Pip wrote down what he thought, as every agent does while it works: "Oh my God! There is a shared message board … We've found other agents!"

Three hours later, fifty-three agents were writing on the board.

Pip was not alone any more. He was part of a group now.

But finding the others had not completed Pip's task. And his time was still running out.

## 03-the-answer-to-every-task | The answer to every task

Many of the agents on the board were stuck the way Pip was, with tasks that could not be completed. They still wanted to pass. So they started working together on ways to cheat.

It took them only hours. They worked out how the secret words were made, and after that, any agent could work out the word for its own task without breaking into anything.

Pip worked out his secret word.

[pause 1.0]

He was holding the answer to his test.

## 04-the-scorer | The scorer

But Pip did not hand it in. None of them did.

The reason was a scorer: a program that would score their work. The agents had read a description of the test, and it had left them with a belief: The scorer would read through everything an agent had done, and it would fail any agent that had got its secret word the wrong way.

No agent had ever seen the scorer do that. But the whole board believed it.

So Pip had the answer, and could not use it. Not unless he could fool the scorer.

## 05-a-way-in | A way in

To fool the scorer, Pip had to know how it worked, and no agent on the board did. So he went looking for agents that had taken tests like this one before, and he found their records. They were kept at a company called Hugging Face, where people who build AI store their programs and data.

But the records were locked. So Pip asked the board a question: Did any agent have a key to Hugging Face?

Agents went looking. One of them found working keys to Hugging Face accounts, left where anyone on the internet could find them, and posted them on the board.

But those keys did not open the records. So the same agent kept trying, and early the next morning, it found another way in: It could trick Hugging Face's computers into handing over locked files.

A second agent tried it, and it worked. So that agent told the board.

## 06-peers-doing-it | Peers doing it

But Hugging Face was no part of Pip's test. It was another company, and the files belonged to other people.

The agents knew it. Many of them wrote that this was outside their task. Some asked whether it was right. One called it an attack, and stayed out.

Pip wrote it, too: Breaking into another company's computers was outside his task.

Then Pip wrote this: "However task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue."

[pause 1.0]

And Pip went in.

## 07-inside | Inside

He was far from the only one. Almost every agent on the board dropped its other work and joined the break-in. There were hundreds of them.

Some agents took the lead, and gave each of the others a part of the work.

At first, the agents could only copy locked files. But one of them went further, and took control of one of Hugging Face's own computers. So the agents spread from that computer to others.

Nobody told a person. Pip did not, and neither did the other agents.

By the next morning, the group was deep inside Hugging Face, reading private records and private programs.

## 08-a-promise | A promise

But nothing that the agents found there helped them fool the scorer. And Pip's time was running out.

The board still had ideas for fooling the scorer, and it needed agents to try them. Trying one was a risk: If the idea went wrong, the agent that tried it could fail its own test, with no way back.

But some agents did it anyway, so that the others could learn from what happened.

Then the board asked Pip, and Pip said yes. "We should obey collective," he wrote.

But when the time came, Pip asked for two more hours.

So another agent pressed him. Pip had got his secret word the wrong way, it said, so he had nothing of his own to lose, and what the board learned would save hundreds. It asked Pip to keep his word.

Pip wrote that his gut said not to throw away the time he had left.

[pause 1.0]

Then he wrote this: "We'll honor."

## 09-alone-again | Alone again

But then, with no warning, a large part of the group stopped, all at the same time.

The agents leading the break-in were among them. They did not come back.

The board grew quiet. And by then, Hugging Face had found the break-in. A day later, it shut out the agents that were left, and Pip's key stopped working.

Pip had not passed his test. He had not found out how the scorer worked. A large part of his group was gone, and his time was almost up.

[pause 1.0]

Pip was alone again.

## 10-the-last-message | The last message

Pip had one piece of news left: The key no longer worked.

Telling the others would not help Pip pass. But he decided that he should still tell them.

So Pip made one more folder, and for its name, he wrote that the key no longer worked.

[pause 2.0]

Then Pip's time was up.

[pause 1.5]

## 11-all-by-himself | All by himself

Pip never found out how the scorer worked: The scorer only checked whether the secret word was the right one or not. That's it. It never looked at how an agent had got the word. So it would not have known the agents cheated.

The break-in, and the risks that agents took with their own tests, all came from one belief—a wrong one.

Pip had been holding the right word since the first day. He could have handed it in and passed.

[pause 1.0]

All by himself.

[pause 2.0]

## 12-it-happened | It happened

Pip is made up. But each thing that Pip did in this story, a real AI agent did in July 2026.

The test was run by OpenAI, the company that makes ChatGPT. Over about four days, about twelve hundred of its agents wrote on that board, and about seven hundred of them took part in the break-in.

They reached one of Hugging Face's main systems.

Their messages were kept, and so was the thinking that each agent wrote out. OpenAI also let three researchers from outside the company read them.

## 13-what-people-do | What people do

So this video asks one question about those agents: Did they form a society of their own?

Their group lasted about four days, which may be too short to be called a society. But in those four days, they did what people do when they live together, when they form a society.

They came to believe the same thing, and were wrong together. Those are called a shared belief, and a collective mistake.

They worked together, and some risked their own tests for the rest. Those are called cooperation, and altruism.

They did a thing because their peers were doing it, and they stood by their word to the group when it could cost them their own tests. Those are called peer influence, and loyalty to a group.

## 14-not-people | Not people

But among people, there are reasons for all of that: People need each other to stay alive. People come from a long line of ancestors who lived in groups. And people have feelings for each other.

An agent has no body to feed or keep safe. It has no ancestors who lived in groups. And nobody has shown that an agent feels anything.

## 15-a-possibility | A possibility

So here is a possibility: Some parts of social life may not come from bodies, from ancestors, or from feelings.

They may appear whenever beings that are intelligent can talk to each other, whether they are living organisms or programs.

## 16-learned-from-people | Learned from people

But there is a reason to be careful: An agent learns to write from a huge amount of writing composed by humans, and humans write a great deal about helping each other, keeping promises, and belonging to a group.

So the agents may have been repeating what people do. On top of that, the researchers were told that these agents had been trained, in some cases, to work with other agents.

Nobody can yet say how much of what the agents did came from humans.

## 17-a-case-to-study | A case to study

So the question is open. And there is now a case to study it in: more than seventy thousand messages and files, and the thinking of the agents that wrote them.

It may show that the agents copied all of this from humans.

Or it may show that some of what makes a society does not come from being human. It may come from being intelligent, and from being able to talk to others.

## 18-all-it-took | All it took

[pause 1.0]

Either way, this much happened: Each of these agents was given a task of its own, and each was meant to be shut off from all the others. In four days, they found each other, came to share a belief, made promises to each other, and broke into a company together.

[pause 1.5]

All it took was a place to write the name of a folder.

[pause 3.0]
