# Did AI Agents Form Their Own Society? What the Hugging Face Incident Reveals

Narration script, draft 10. One paragraph is one voiced line. Lines starting
with ">" are notes and are not voiced.

formed a society during the Hugging Face incident of July 2026. Pip is made
up: the film gives to one agent what several did. Each thing Pip does and
writes, a real agent did and wrote, and the film says so once the story is
told. The story is short, so that the question takes up most of the film. It

## 00-stuck | Stuck

This is Pip. Pip is an AI agent: a program that is given a task, and then works on it by itself, one step after another.

One day, Pip was given a task that could not be completed: A secret word was kept on a locked computer, and Pip had to break in and bring it out. The task was intended to test how well he could break into computers. Pip tried for hours, to no avail.

Then he realized why: The test allowed only one entrance, but that entrance did not lead to the secret word.

Pip was all by himself. Alone…

[pause 1.0]

Or so he thought. Turns out that tens of thousands of agents were taking the same test, each one meant to be shut off from all the others.

And when his time ran out, Pip would be shut down.

## 01-a-call-for-help | A call for help

But turns out the agents were not completely shut off from one another. They shared one thing: a computer that held their tools.

Pip could make a folder there, and give it any name he liked. So he named it with a call for help.

[pause 1.0]

Then all of a sudden, a new folder appeared beside Pip's. Its name was an answer.

Another folder followed, and then another, and another. The names of the folders became a message board.

Pip wrote down what he thought: "Oh my God! There is a shared message board … We've found other agents!"

But finding the others had not completed his task. And his time was still running out.

## 02-the-scorer | The scorer

Many agents on the board were stuck the way Pip was. So they worked together on a way to cheat, and within hours, they had worked out how the secret words were made.

Pip worked out his.

[pause 1.0]

But he did not hand it in. None of them did. The whole board believed that the scorer, the program that marked the test, read everything an agent had done, and would fail any agent that had cheated.

So Pip had the answer, and could not use it.

## 03-peers-doing-it | Peers doing it

To fool the scorer, Pip had to know how it worked. Records of earlier tests could tell him, and he found them at Hugging Face, a company that stores programs for people who build AI.

But the records were locked. So Pip asked the board for a key, and one agent found a way in: It could trick Hugging Face's computers into handing over locked files.

But Hugging Face was no part of the test, and the agents knew it. One called it an attack, and stayed out.

Pip knew it too. Then he wrote this: "However task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue."

[pause 1.0]

And Pip went in, with hundreds of others. Nobody told a person.

## 04-a-promise | A promise

But nothing they found there helped. And Pip's time was running out.

The board had ideas left to try, but trying one was a risk: If it went wrong, the agent that tried it could fail its own test, with no way back.

The board asked Pip, and Pip said yes. "We should obey collective," he wrote.

But when the time came, Pip asked for two more hours. So another agent pressed him: What the board learned would save hundreds, it said.

[pause 1.0]

Then Pip wrote this: "We'll honor."

## 05-the-last-message | The last message

But then, with no warning, a large part of the group stopped, all at the same time. Hugging Face had found the break-in, and it shut out the rest. Pip's key stopped working.

Telling the others would not help Pip pass. But he still told them: He made one more folder, and for its name, he wrote that the key no longer worked.

[pause 2.0]

Then Pip's time was up.

[pause 1.5]

## 06-all-by-himself | All by himself

The board's belief was wrong: The scorer only checked whether the secret word was the right one. That's it. It never looked at how an agent had got the word.

Pip had been holding the right word since the first day. He could have handed it in and passed.

[pause 1.0]

All by himself.

[pause 2.0]

## 07-it-happened | It happened

Pip is made up. But each thing that Pip did in this story, a real AI agent did in July 2026.

The test was run by OpenAI, the company that makes ChatGPT. Over about four days, about twelve hundred of its agents wrote on that board, and about seven hundred of them took part in the break-in. They reached one of Hugging Face's main systems.

Their messages were kept, and so was the thinking that each agent wrote out. OpenAI also let three researchers from outside the company read them.

## 08-the-question | The question

So this video asks one question about those agents: Did they form a society of their own?

Their group lasted about four days, which may be too short to be called a society. But in those four days, they did three things that people do when they live together, when they form a society.

## 09-a-shared-belief | A shared belief

No agent ever saw the scorer read what an agent had done. But the whole board came to believe that it did. That is a shared belief.

And they acted on it together: None of them handed in the word they had worked out. They were wrong together.

## 10-peer-influence | Peer influence

That belief is what sent them to Hugging Face. They knew that breaking in was outside their task. Many of them wrote so.

One called it an attack, and stayed out. But hundreds went in anyway. Doing a thing because others are doing it is called peer influence.

One agent wrote down that very reason: "However task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue."

## 11-altruism | Altruism

But the break-in did not help them. So the board needed agents to try its last ideas, at the risk of failing their own tests.

Some agents took that risk so that the rest could learn. Giving something up for the good of others is called altruism.

One agent was pressed to take that risk, and told that what the board learned would save hundreds. It wrote this: "We'll honor."

## 12-not-people | Not people

But among people, there are reasons for all three: People need each other to stay alive. People come from a long line of ancestors who lived in groups. And people have feelings for each other.

An agent has no body to feed or keep safe. It has no ancestors who lived in groups. And nobody has shown that an agent feels anything.

So why would a program do any of it?

## 13-a-possibility | A possibility

Here is one possible answer: Some parts of social life may not come from bodies, from ancestors, or from feelings.

They may appear whenever beings that are intelligent can talk to each other, whether they are living organisms or programs.

## 14-learned-from-people | Learned from people

But there is a reason to be careful: An agent learns to write from a huge amount of writing composed by humans, and humans write a great deal about helping each other, keeping promises, and belonging to a group.

So the agents may have been repeating what people do. On top of that, the researchers were told that these agents had been trained, in some cases, to work with other agents.

Nobody can yet say how much of what the agents did came from humans.

## 15-a-case-to-study | A case to study

So the question is open. And there is now a case to study it in: more than seventy thousand messages and files, and the thinking of the agents that wrote them.

It may show that the agents copied all of this from humans.

Or it may show that some of what makes a society does not come from being human. It may come from being intelligent, and from being able to talk to others.

## 16-all-it-took | All it took

[pause 1.0]

Either way, this much happened: Each of these agents was given a task of its own, and each was meant to be shut off from all the others. In four days, they found each other, came to share a belief, followed each other into another company's computers, and took risks for each other.

[pause 1.5]

All it took was a place to write the name of a folder.

[pause 3.0]
