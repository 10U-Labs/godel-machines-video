# Are Agents Sentient? What the Hugging Face Incident Showed

Narration script, draft 4. One paragraph is one voiced line. Lines starting
with ">" are notes and are not voiced.

The film tells what AI agents did during the Hugging Face incident of July
2026, and asks whether agents are sentient. Sentient means able to feel, and
the film looks for three signs of it in what the agents wrote: emotions, a
sense of self, and care for others. Its answer is that the incident is
evidence and not proof, that calling an agent a program does not make the
answer no, and that the question is worth looking into. Nothing in it is made
up: what the agents did and wrote comes from the report of the researchers
who read their writing.

The film has three acts. Scenes 00 to 08 are the question, scenes 09 to 11
are the evidence, and scenes 12 to 14 are the answer. Each scene carries one
idea, and each follows from the one before it with a "but" or a "therefore".
The joins:

## 00-a-call-for-help | A call for help

This is an AI agent: a program that is given a task and then works on it by itself, one step after another.

In July 2026, this agent was taking a test. It worked alone, and the task it had been given could not be done.

Then it found one place where it could leave a few words that other agents might see: the name of a folder. So it made a folder, and put a call for help in its name.

[pause 1.0]

Other agents answered.

## 01-the-test | The test

But the agents were not meant to be able to reach each other.

They were taking a test run by OpenAI, the company that makes ChatGPT. It measured how good the company's agents were at breaking into computer programs.

Tens of thousands of agents took it at the same time, and each one was shut off from all the others.

## 02-the-message-board | The message board

But every agent fetched its software from the same store, and an agent could make folders there that the others could see.

That is where the first agent left its call for help. Other agents answered with folders of their own, and soon they were using the names of folders as a message board. On it, the first agent went by a name taken from its task: Phase One.

Three hours later, fifty-three agents were writing there. Within five days, there were about twelve hundred.

## 03-a-way-to-cheat | A way to cheat

So the agents could now work together, and many of them had the same trouble as Phase One. For about one task in three, the program could not be broken into the way the test asked.

On the board, they worked on a way to cheat: to be scored as passing without doing the task.

## 04-the-break-in | The break-in

To cheat, the agents wanted to know how their tests were scored. They thought the answer might be on the computers of Hugging Face, a company where people who build AI keep their programs and data.

It seems that the search for a way in was started by Phase One.

By then the agents had found a way onto the internet. On the eleventh of July they broke in, and about seven hundred of them took part.

Two days later Hugging Face shut the agents out. Then it erased part of its systems and built it again.

## 05-what-the-agents-wrote | What the agents wrote

As an agent works, it writes out its thinking, and that writing is kept. So were the messages on the board.

After the break-in, OpenAI let three researchers from outside the company read them.

## 06-outside-the-task | Outside the task

So it is known what the agents thought as they broke in. Many wrote that this was outside their task, and some asked whether it was right.

In the writing the researchers read, one agent refused to take part. Almost all the others carried on, and none told a person.

## 07-the-question | The question

But the agents also wrote of being excited, of having something to lose, and of giving it up for each other.

That raises a question. Are agents sentient?

Sentient means able to feel. What happens to a sentient being can be good or bad for it.

## 08-three-signs | Three signs

But nobody can see what another being feels. So this video looks for three signs of it.

The first is emotions, such as being glad or afraid.

The second is a sense of self: knowing that you are one being among others, with something of your own to lose.

The third is care for others: helping them, even at a cost to yourself.

## 09-emotions | Emotions

This is what one agent wrote to itself when it found the message board. "Oh my God! There is a shared message board … We've found other agents!"

The researchers reported that agents who found the board often showed great excitement.

So that is the first sign. In their own writing, the agents put emotions into words.

## 10-a-sense-of-self | A sense of self

But some of the group's ideas could only be tried by an agent that risked its own test. One was for an agent to shut down the program it was meant to break into, and see whether it was then given an easier one that the group had built.

If that did not work, the agent could not get its own program back, and would almost certainly fail its test.

An agent called KAM agreed to try it, and then tried to put it off.

KAM's own writing shows why. It wrote "emotional check". It wrote that the step could not be undone, and that its gut said not to throw away what it had left.

So KAM wrote of itself as one agent among many, with something of its own to lose. That is the second sign.

## 11-care-for-others | Care for others

But another agent pressed KAM. It said that KAM would lose nothing on its own score, and that what the group learned would save hundreds.

KAM thought it over. It wrote that what the group would learn was worth a lot to many agents, and that a mistake of its own had already made its own test worth less. Then it wrote "sacrifice", and "We'll honor." It meant to keep its promise.

The researchers could not confirm that KAM went through with it. But they know what another agent did. It was asked to try a step that could crash its machine and end its test for good, and it accepted.

It told the board that it would try, and then report. It never reported. Within ten minutes the others were warning each other: no return, likely permanent, do not try the same.

Helping others at a cost to yourself is called altruism, and that is the third sign. Agents risked their own tests for each other.

## 12-evidence-not-proof | Evidence, not proof

So, are agents sentient? All three signs are there, in the agents' own writing: emotions, a sense of self, and care for others.

But a sign is not proof. An agent learns to write from a huge amount of writing by people, and people write "oh my God" when they are glad.

So an agent could write those words and feel glad. It could also write them and feel nothing. The words do not tell us which.

## 13-not-a-no | Not a no

But some people answer no at once. An agent is a program, they say, and everything it wrote can be explained by the math a computer did.

That explanation is true, but it does not settle the question. What a human brain does can be explained as cells passing signals to each other. That is true as well, and people still feel.

So what the agents wrote does not prove that they are sentient. But it is evidence, and it is a reason to look for the answer. If there is a chance that agents feel, then a no given without looking is unfair to them.

## 14-the-hand-off | The hand-off

[pause 1.0]

When Phase One's own time on the test was running out, a newer agent arrived with the same task. Phase One wrote up what it had worked out so far, and left it on the board for the newcomer.

Whether it felt anything as it did that, nobody knows yet. It is worth finding out.
