# Only the four concepts

The Gödel machines film is about recursive self-improvement, the Gödel Machine, the Darwin Gödel Machine, and the Huxley-Gödel Machine, and nothing else. This memory holds for that film alone. On 2026-11-14, after hearing script draft 7, the user said the scene on I. J. Good's 1965 idea was material they had not asked for, that they had said plainly the film covers only those four things, and that nothing needs its fine detail explained: not that the Gödel Machine needs proofs, not who Kurt Gödel was or what he is known for. They also called Good's "last invention" line valueless.

**Why:** Claude's interpretation: background, history, and mechanism detail pull attention from the four concepts the viewer is meant to leave with.

**How to apply:** Explain each concept at the level of the user's phrases: the Gödel Machine improves itself, the Darwin Gödel Machine improves itself through direct descendants, the Huxley-Gödel Machine improves itself through clades. Leave out history (Good, Turing, Bletchley), how each machine decides, where names come from, and who the namesakes were, unless the user asks. Before adding any line, ask whether it explains one of the four concepts; if not, cut it. Also check each line against the others for contradictions in wording (draft 7 said there would be only one Pip, then called each changed Pip "a new one").
