# Every production tells a story

A production has to tell a story, hook the audience, and have what the user calls soul. It is the user's rule, and it holds for every production (see [The repo is a media factory](the-repo-is-a-media-factory.md)). A script that is accurate and in textbook order, with no people or stakes in it, is only passable.

The joins between scenes are tested with the rule that Trey Parker and Matt Stone use for story outlines: between any two beats the joining word is "but" or "therefore", and never "and then". A scene that can only be joined with "and then" is cut, merged, or given a cause. The user put this test forward; that it fits here is Claude's judgement. In the Gödel machines film the joins are written out at the top of `script/script.md`.

**Why:** No further reason comes with the hook. Claude's interpretation: a correct explanation does not hold a general audience by itself; viewers need someone to follow, a question, and a payoff.

The hook is the opening itself. The user's rule: a script whose opening has no hook is boring, however well it keeps the wording rules. An opening that reports an event from outside, stops for a definition, and leaves the production's question to a later scene has no hook.

**How to apply:** Give the viewer someone to follow, a question, and a payoff, and apply the same test to picture, music, and voice. Claude's interpretation of the hook, not confirmed: the first voiced line puts the viewer with one someone at one moment, such as a program writing down why it goes on with a break-in; a term is explained after that line and not before it; and the production's question is asked before the opening scene ends. Past the opening, a scene ends on what happened or on someone's own words, never on a sentence that only defines a term. When a script argues from things to be found, it tells the viewer what to look for before the evidence, in at most three things, and the answer counts them off. Test new or reordered scenes with the joining words. The hook comes from the story, not from hype words; see [State facts plainly](state-facts-plainly.md). Keep each scene to one idea; see [One idea at a time](one-idea-at-a-time.md). The wording rule is [Plain everyday words](plain-everyday-words.md), and the story stays literal; see [No analogies in script](no-analogies-in-script.md). What a production's story is about is settled for each production; for the Gödel machines film it is in [The Gödel film's subject and order](godel-film-subject-and-order.md). For that film the user allows made-up stories and characters. Claude's interpretation, not confirmed: the same holds for other productions, as long as the narration says what is made up.
