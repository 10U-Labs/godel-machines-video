# State facts plainly

The narration states what happened in concrete terms, and does not tell the viewer how to feel about it. It is the user's rule, and it holds for every production (see [The repo is a media factory](the-repo-is-a-media-factory.md)). Two faults break it. One is a word that hypes: a line that calls a program's job "strange". The other is a phrase that points at an event without saying what it was: a line that says researchers pointed a program at "a problem of its own" leaves the viewer unable to tell what happened.

**Why:** No further reason comes with the rule. Claude's interpretation: the hook the user wants (see [Every production tells a story](every-production-tells-a-story.md)) has to come from the facts themselves. A word that hypes an event makes a production sound as if it is selling something, and a phrase that points at an event without saying what it was leaves a general viewer behind.

**How to apply:** Leave out adjectives and asides whose only job is to make something sound remarkable, such as "strange", "unusual", "takes no chances", and a bare claim that an event really took place. Say who did what to what: name the fault, the task, the result. Replace idioms that stand in for an event, such as "ran into the same wall" and "went nowhere", with the event. Go through the whole script for both faults on every draft, not only the lines the user points at. The rule covers text on screen as well. It sits beside [Plain everyday words](plain-everyday-words.md) and [No analogies in script](no-analogies-in-script.md).
