# Every word has a purpose

Each word of a narration script does a job. It is the user's rule for scripts. A word, a clause, or a line that does no job is cut, however well it reads.

**Why:** No reason comes with the rule. Claude's interpretation: a viewer hears each line once and cannot skip, so a clause that carries nothing costs attention that the story needs.

**How to apply:** Ask of each clause what the viewer loses without it, and cut it when the answer is nothing. Claude's interpretation, not confirmed: the rule holds for every production's script; a clause earns its place by moving the story, by telling the viewer something that a later line depends on, or by carrying the feeling of the moment; and a name given to a thing counts as a job only where a later line cannot be understood without it. In the society film, a clause in which Pip names his group goes, because the line already says that he is part of a group, the narration can go on saying the group, and the one quotation that uses the name is plain where it stands.
