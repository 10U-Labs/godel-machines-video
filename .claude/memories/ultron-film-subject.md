# The Ultron film's subject

This memory holds for the Ultron film alone, and what it sets is the user's direction. The film asks whether Ultron is a self-improving AI, and answers yes. Its Ultron is the one in the 2015 film Avengers: Age of Ultron, not the one in the comics.

Of that film's plot, three steps matter, and they are simple. Ultron starts as an AI with no body. He turns himself into a feeble robot. Then he turns himself into a powerful one. The retelling is short: a script that gives the plot a third of its running time gives it far too much. The step to the powerful body takes one sentence, which says that Ultron eventually built himself a better body; how the first body was lost and where the second was built are details the script does not need. What Ultron did is told in the past tense.

The question is a rhetorical one: the script asks whether Ultron is a self-improving AI, then says yes and gives the reasons. The term is the user's choice, made after the papers were read for it. It is the term in the title of Stephen Omohundro's 2007 paper The Nature of Self-Improving Artificial Intelligence; the body of that paper, and of his 2008 paper The Basic AI Drives, says "self-improving systems". In those papers such a system alters its own software or its own physical structure so that it meets its goals better, and the 2007 paper names the two kinds informational and physical self-improvement. Ultron alters his physical structure, his body, and leaves his code as it is, and the script names what he does by the paper's term, physical self-improvement. The user chose this concept because it is not limited to a machine that improves its code.

The Gödel Machine has no place in the script, not even as a comparison. A Gödel Machine improves by rewriting its code, so a question about it has a plain no for an answer, and the film needs a question it can answer with a yes. This is the user's direction.

**Why:** No reason comes with the direction. Claude's interpretation: the three forms are the self-improvement that the title's question is about, and every other part of the plot pulls a general viewer away from it.

**How to apply:** Tell Ultron's story as those three forms, each one made by Ultron himself, and give each as a reason for the yes. Leave out the rest of the plot unless the user asks for it: the scepter and its gem, what Ultron wants and why, the twins, the vibranium, the body that becomes Vision, the copies of himself, the flying city, and how he ends. A line names the film. The two heroes who try to make him and the heroes who destroy his first body are left out too, unless the user asks for them.

Each step is checked against the film before it is narrated or shown, without source notes in the script, per [Script holds only narration](script-holds-only-narration.md). The film's own events are told as the film's: the narration says that Ultron is from a film, as [Every production tells a story](every-production-tells-a-story.md) asks of anything made up. What a self-improving AI is gets checked against Omohundro's two papers, as the first film's facts are checked against theirs (see [The Gödel film's subject and order](godel-film-subject-and-order.md)).
