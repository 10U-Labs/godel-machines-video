# Show people, not panels

A production's pictures are illustrated scenes with people in them, not diagrams of panels, labels, and meters. When the narration mentions people, such as researchers or a named scientist, the pictures show them. It is the user's rule, and it holds for every production (see [The repo is a media factory](the-repo-is-a-media-factory.md)).

**Why:** The user's reason is that a production is made for people, and that scenes without them are boring in both story and picture. Claude's interpretation: scenes built from program windows, text panels, meters, and on-screen captions that repeat the narration read as a slide deck, not an animated film, and they leave out the people who carry the story.

**How to apply:** Draw illustrated scenes: people, places, depth, a moving camera, and constant small motion. Show the people the narration names, in the places where things happened. Use on-screen text sparingly and do not caption what the voice is already saying.
